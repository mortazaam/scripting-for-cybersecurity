Lab 7 Findings

Accounts targeted by failed logins, checked with account_source.sh:
admin, root, guest, test, backup, oracle - these are generic account names,
not real staff names. That suggests the attacker was guessing standard
names rather than using inside knowledge of the organisation.

Legacy accounts found only in the old backup list are the most dangerous
if they still exist on the server, because nobody is monitoring them and
they may still have old permissions.

Config findings from debug_check.sh and tls_check.sh:
the backup configuration has debug mode on. Debug in production exposes
stack traces and internal paths, and sometimes credentials, in error pages.
Some configs do not mention TLS at all, which means traffic may not be
encrypted.

port_check.sh showed config-old.txt uses port 80 and config-new.txt uses
443. Both are privileged ports (under 1024) so only root can bind them.

The accounts with plaintext passwords stored on the workstation and
successful logins are worth reporting together - that combination is the
most likely way the attacker got in.
